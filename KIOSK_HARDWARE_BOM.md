# CampusPrint Kiosk - Commercial Hardware Bill of Materials (BOM)

This document outlines the required hardware components to transition the CampusPrint Kiosk from a functional prototype to a robust, scalable, and commercially deployable product. These components are specifically chosen to ensure maximum uptime, remote manageability, and physical security in high-traffic university environments.

## Core Computing & Interface
These components form the brain and user interface of the kiosk.

1. **Raspberry Pi (Core Processor)**
   - **Purpose:** Acts as the central computer managing the kiosk web application, communicating with the cloud backend (Supabase), and sending print jobs to the printer.
   - **Commercial Rationale:** Cost-effective, low power consumption, and highly customizable for IoT deployments.
2. **Solid State Drive (SSD) via USB/NVMe HAT**
   - **Purpose:** Replaces traditional MicroSD cards for booting the operating system and storing temporary print files.
   - **Commercial Rationale:** MicroSD cards degrade quickly under continuous read/write cycles and corrupt easily upon sudden power loss. An SSD provides enterprise-grade durability and significantly faster boot/processing times.
3. **16-Inch Capacitive Touch Display**
   - **Purpose:** The primary user interface for students to upload, preview, and pay for their print jobs.
   - **Commercial Rationale:** A 16-inch screen is large enough for clear document previews but compact enough to maintain a small kiosk footprint. Capacitive touch ensures a modern, smartphone-like responsiveness.

## Printing & Hardware Interaction
The mechanical components handling the physical output.

4. **Commercial-Grade Printer**
   - **Purpose:** Executes the actual printing of documents.
   - **Commercial Rationale:** Must support high-volume output with reliable paper-feed mechanisms to minimize paper jams and maintenance calls.
5. **RFID Reader**
   - **Purpose:** Enables quick authentication for students using their university ID cards or key fobs.
   - **Commercial Rationale:** Reduces friction in the user journey. Instead of typing credentials on a public screen, a simple tap logs the user in securely and instantly.

## Power & Environmental Management
Critical infrastructure to keep the machine running 24/7 without on-site technicians.

6. **Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) Battery Backup**
   - **Purpose:** Provides a temporary power buffer if the main wall power is kicked out or disrupted.
   - **Commercial Rationale:** Sudden power cuts mid-print will cause severe paper jams and can corrupt the OS file system. A UPS allows the kiosk to gracefully pause/cancel the print job, safely spin down the SSD, and shut off without hardware damage.
7. **Active Cooling System (Fans & Vents)**
   - **Purpose:** Dissipates heat generated by the Raspberry Pi, printer, and display.
   - **Commercial Rationale:** Continuous operation in an enclosed chassis creates significant heat buildup, which can thermal-throttle the CPU (slowing down the UI) or cause premature hardware failure.
8. **Heavy-Duty Surge Protector / Power Distribution Unit (PDU)**
   - **Purpose:** Safely splits a single wall outlet to power the Printer, Display, and Pi simultaneously.
   - **Commercial Rationale:** Protects the sensitive electronics from power surges and spikes common in large institutional buildings.

## Connectivity & Remote Management
Infrastructure required to operate a fleet of kiosks from a central office.

9. **Ethernet Connection (Primary) OR 4G/LTE IoT Modem (Fallback)**
   - **Purpose:** Provides the critical link to the Supabase cloud backend for file retrieval, payment processing, and real-time status updates.
   - **Commercial Rationale:** If the university IT department allows it, a hardwired Ethernet connection is the absolute gold standard for reliability. If the campus network is restricted (e.g., MAC filtering, captive portals), a dedicated 4G IoT modem ensures the kiosk brings its own independent, unblockable internet connection.
10. **Remote Device Management (MDM) Software (e.g., BalenaOS)**
    - **Purpose:** Background software running on the Pi that connects to a central dashboard.
    - **Commercial Rationale:** Allows the engineering team to remotely SSH into the kiosk, push software updates over-the-air (OTA), and force-reboot the machine without dispatching a technician.

## Chassis & Physical Security
Protecting the internal components from tampering and theft.

11. **Custom Aluminum Enclosure**
    - **Purpose:** Houses all components securely while providing a sleek, branded aesthetic.
    - **Commercial Rationale:** Aluminum is lightweight yet highly durable, dissipates heat well, and presents a premium look that universities demand for their campuses.
12. **Keyed Cam Locks & Concealed I/O**
    - **Purpose:** Secures the internal chassis doors and paper trays.
    - **Commercial Rationale:** Prevents theft of paper, ink, and computing hardware. Concealing all USB/Ethernet ports ensures malicious actors cannot plug in keyboards or devices to hack the kiosk software.
13. **Security Anchors (Floor/Desk Bolts or Kensington Locks)**
    - **Purpose:** Physically secures the entire kiosk structure to its environment.
    - **Commercial Rationale:** Prevents the entire unit from being tipped over or stolen from unsupervised areas like libraries or student unions.
